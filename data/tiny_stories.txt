The little robot kept a notebook beside the window. Each morning it wrote down one new idea.
On Monday the robot learned to count the birds. On Tuesday it learned to listen to the rain.
A friend asked the robot how it learned. The robot said that every small mistake was a clue.
They built a paper bridge across a puddle. The first bridge fell, but the second bridge stood.
The notebook held both designs. Keeping the mistakes helped them understand the better bridge.
At sunset they returned to the workshop. A warm lamp lit the table and the unfinished drawings.
The robot opened its notebook and wrote a sentence. A good question can begin a useful experiment.
The next morning a blue bird landed on the windowsill. It carried a thin thread in its beak.
The robot watched the bird build a nest. One small piece at a time became a home in the tree.
Its friend brought a box of wooden blocks. Together they sorted the blocks by shape and size.
Some blocks formed a tower. Other blocks formed a road. The smallest block became a little door.
They changed one piece and watched what happened. When the tower leaned, they made its base wider.
The robot wrote the result in its notebook. Testing an idea was more useful than guessing alone.
Later they read a story about a boat. The boat crossed a quiet lake beneath a silver moon.
The friend wondered where the boat would go next. The robot imagined a harbor full of yellow lights.
They wrote a different ending for the story. In their ending the boat carried seeds to an island.
Spring arrived, and the seeds grew into flowers. The island became a place for travelers to rest.
The robot closed the notebook and looked outside. There was still a great deal left to learn.
Rain returned to the workshop roof. The friends listened and counted the drops against the glass.
Tomorrow they would try another experiment. Tonight they were happy to have learned something small.
